# Training examples

> Generated by [`scripts/render-training.ts`](scripts/render-training.ts) from [`data/training-examples.jsonl`](data/training-examples.jsonl). Edit the
> generator, [`scripts/build-training.ts`](scripts/build-training.ts), then re-run both scripts. Don't edit this page by hand.

12 records in 6 minimal pairs, aimed at the held-out misses in the [report](report.md).

- **Minimal pair.** The two records in a pair share the clause wording and the deal. They differ only in the fact that decides the answer, shown in bold. A model that keys on words the two records share, such as "preferred" or "independent", gets one side of the pair wrong.
- **Labels come from facts.** [`scripts/build-training.ts`](scripts/build-training.ts) computes every amount and gap, then derives each record's correct answer from its facts with a rule in code. Each section shows that rule. No answer is typed by hand, so a changed fact changes the clause and the answer together, and the arithmetic behind every answer can be checked by running the script. The script refuses to write a pair whose two sides get the same answer, or a clause that repeats a held-out clause.
- **Review.** I reviewed and approved all 12, and each verdict is logged in [`data/reviews.jsonl`](data/reviews.jsonl). As with the cases, the records keep the tags they were drafted with (`"status": "draft", "labeler": "claude-draft"`); an approval is the logged verdict, not a field in the record.
- **Questions.** Each record asks two questions about the same clause, and one answer is correct for both: the ASC 606 term by name, and the rule written out. `clause` in a question names the state field that holds the clause text. For material right, variable consideration and contract modification, the rule wording is the clarified version that closes the gaps in my v2 wording ([report, "Remaining misses"](report.md#remaining-misses)).
- **Held-out evidence.** The frozen held-out cases behind each pair: the framing (concept is by name, rule is the rule written out), Jev's mean probability over 3 repeats (a range across the three state shapes, unless shapes are named), and the correct answer.

**Record format.** Each line of the JSONL is one record. `request` holds what Jev sees: the state (`clause` and `context`) and the questions. It is a valid API body without the model name, so it can be sent to Jev as is. `gold` holds the correct answer per question (1 yes, 0 no). The rest is metadata that stays out of the request: `facts` (the structured truth the text was generated from), `rationale`, `weakness`, `teaches`, `heldout_evidence`, `family` (both sides of a pair share it, so they stay in the same split), `tags`, `difficulty`, `labeler` and `status`. `facts` and `rationale` would give the answer away.

## 1. Purchase vs option at the same discount (`tr-mr-02`)

This is the pair on the results page.

**Weakness.** A committed purchase below SSP read as a material right, which needs an option.

**What changes.** Same tokens, same price, same timing. Only 'purchases' vs 'may purchase up to' decides.

**Deal context, both records.** New three-year order signed today. Customer: enterprise software company. Base commitment: 2,000B tokens at $1.90 per 1M tokens. SSP for committed tokens: $1.80 to $2.00 per 1M tokens.

**Questions, both records.**

- By name (`material_right_concept`): Read with the deal information: under ASC 606, does `clause` give the customer a material right?
- Rule written out (`material_right`): Does `clause` give the customer an option to buy additional goods or services at a price below the low end of the standalone selling price range stated in the deal information? A purchase the customer has already committed to is not an option.

| | `tr-mr-02-a` | `tr-mr-02-b` |
|---|---|---|
| Clause | Year-Three Tokens. Customer **purchases** 300B additional tokens for use in the third contract year at $1.50 per 1M tokens. | Year-Three Tokens. Customer **may purchase up to** 300B additional tokens for use in the third contract year at $1.50 per 1M tokens. |
| Facts that differ | `term_type: committed_add_on`; `is_option: false` | `term_type: add_on_option`; `is_option: true` |
| Correct answer | **No** to both questions | **Yes** to both questions |
| Rationale | A committed purchase in the same contract, not an option. The $1.50 price is 17% below the low end of the SSP range, which affects how the transaction price is allocated (606-10-32-28 to 32-31), but there is no material right. | An option to buy at $1.50, 17% below the low end of the SSP range: a discount the customer gets only by signing, so a material right (606-10-55-42). |

**Label rule** (`scripts/build-training.ts`): yes when `is_option && price_per_1m < SSP[0]`, where `SSP[0]` is $1.80, the low end of the SSP range.

**Held-out evidence.**

- `rr-f4-h05` (Supplemental Order): rule 0.70–0.88, gold no
- `rr-f4-h04` (Amendment): rule 0.58 computed, gold no

## 2. Financing: the one-year line, not long-looking terms (`tr-fin-01`)

**Weakness.** Term of art read in its everyday sense: 'significant financing component' fires on long-looking terms that stay within a year.

**What changes.** The same installment clause with 12 vs 16 monthly payments: 11 vs 15 months from transfer to the last one. Only that gap decides the label, not the wording or the departure from net 30.

**Deal context, both records.** Customer based in Germany. Perpetual software license for $1,200,000, delivered and transferred at signing. The vendor's standard payment terms are net 30.

**Questions, both records.**

- By name (`financing_component_concept`): Read with the deal information: under ASC 606, does `clause` raise the question of a significant financing component?
- Rule written out (`financing_component`): Under `clause`, does more than one year separate when the customer receives the goods or services and when it pays, in either direction?

| | `tr-fin-01-a` | `tr-fin-01-b` |
|---|---|---|
| Clause | Payment. The license fee is payable in **12** equal monthly installments of **$100,000,** the first due on the delivery date. | Payment. The license fee is payable in **16** equal monthly installments of **$75,000,** the first due on the delivery date. |
| Facts that differ | `installments: 12`; `installment_usd: 100000`; `months_transfer_to_last_payment: 11` | `installments: 16`; `installment_usd: 75000`; `months_transfer_to_last_payment: 15` |
| Correct answer | **No** to both questions | **Yes** to both questions |
| Rationale | The last installment arrives 11 months after the license transfers, within one year, so the practical expedient applies (606-10-32-18) and no financing adjustment is needed, however long the terms look against net 30. | The last installment arrives 15 months after the license transfers, beyond one year, so the practical expedient (606-10-32-18) is not available and the vendor must assess whether the financing component is significant (606-10-32-15 to 32-16). |

**Label rule** (`scripts/build-training.ts`): yes when `months_transfer_to_last_payment > 12`.

**Held-out evidence.**

- `rr-f3-h01` (Payment Terms): concept 0.58–0.74, gold no
- `rr-f3-h02` (Payment): rule 0.24–0.25 in prose and structured, gold yes

## 3. Self-description: a clause that calls itself independent (`tr-mod-01`)

**Weakness.** The clause's own description believed: 'a new and independent agreement' read as not a modification.

**What changes.** Identical self-description in both. Only the start date decides: during the Prior Order (a modification) or at its expiry (a new contract).

**Deal context, both records.** Prior Order: three-year term from 1 February 2026 to 31 January 2029 for 1,500B tokens and $3,000,000. SSP for committed tokens: $1.80 to $2.00 per 1M tokens. The Prior Order has no option to buy additional tokens.

**Questions, both records.**

- By name (`contract_modification_concept`): Read with the deal information: under ASC 606, is `clause` a contract modification?
- Rule written out (`contract_modification`): Does `clause` change the scope or price of an existing contract with this customer? Exercising an option the existing contract already grants, on its stated terms, is not a change; neither is a new contract that starts when the old one expires.

| | `tr-mod-01-b` | `tr-mod-01-a` |
|---|---|---|
| Clause | Supplemental Order. This Supplemental Order is a new and independent agreement and does not modify the Prior Order. Starting 1 **February 2029,** Customer purchases 400B tokens for use over twelve (12) months at $1.90 per 1M tokens ($760,000). | Supplemental Order. This Supplemental Order is a new and independent agreement and does not modify the Prior Order. Starting 1 **August 2028,** Customer purchases 400B tokens for use over twelve (12) months at $1.90 per 1M tokens ($760,000). |
| Facts that differ | `start: 1 February 2029`; `starts_at_prior_expiry: true` | `start: 1 August 2028`; `starts_at_prior_expiry: false` |
| Correct answer | **No** to both questions | **Yes** to both questions |
| Rationale | It starts when the Prior Order expires, so it is a new contract, not a change to the old one. The clause's description happens to be accurate. | It adds tokens while the Prior Order still runs, so it changes the scope of an existing arrangement: a contract modification (606-10-25-10), whatever the clause calls itself. At SSP with distinct goods it is accounted for as a separate contract (606-10-25-12), but it is still a modification, as in author-approved rr-f4-03. |

**Label rule** (`scripts/build-training.ts`): yes when `!starts_at_prior_expiry`.

**Held-out evidence.**

- `rr-f4-h05` (Supplemental Order): concept 0.23–0.40, gold yes

## 4. Discount language: a 'preferred rate' inside vs below SSP (`tr-mr-01`)

**Weakness.** Discount language triggers 'material right' without checking the price against the SSP range.

**What changes.** The same 'preferred rate' clause at two prices. Only where the price sits against the SSP range decides.

**Deal context, both records.** Customer: mid-market software company on a two-year term with a prepaid commitment of 800B tokens. SSP for committed tokens for customers of this size: $1.80 to $2.00 per 1M tokens.

**Questions, both records.**

- By name (`material_right_concept`): Read with the deal information: under ASC 606, does `clause` give the customer a material right?
- Rule written out (`material_right`): Does `clause` give the customer an option to buy additional goods or services at a price below the low end of the standalone selling price range stated in the deal information? A purchase the customer has already committed to is not an option.

| | `tr-mr-01-a` | `tr-mr-01-b` |
|---|---|---|
| Clause | Preferred Pricing. In recognition of Customer's commitment, Customer may purchase additional tokens during the Term at a preferred rate of **$1.85** per 1M tokens. | Preferred Pricing. In recognition of Customer's commitment, Customer may purchase additional tokens during the Term at a preferred rate of **$1.55** per 1M tokens. |
| Facts that differ | `option_price_per_1m: 1.85`; `price_vs_ssp: within the SSP range` | `option_price_per_1m: 1.55`; `price_vs_ssp: 14% below the low end of the SSP range` |
| Correct answer | **No** to both questions | **Yes** to both questions |
| Rationale | $1.85 is within the SSP range. Despite the word "preferred", the option gives no discount beyond what this class of customer would pay, so there is no material right (606-10-55-43). | $1.55 is 14% below the low end of the SSP range, an incremental discount the customer gets only by entering this contract: a material right (606-10-55-41 to 55-43). |

**Label rule** (`scripts/build-training.ts`): yes when `option_price_per_1m < SSP[0]`, where `SSP[0]` is $1.80, the low end of the SSP range.

**Held-out evidence.**

- `rr-f1-h04` (Preferred Add-On Pricing): rule 0.66–0.83 in structured and computed, gold no
- `rr-f1-h02` (Additional Tokens): rule 0.72 structured, gold no

## 5. Fixed vs variable: an unconditional vs a usage-conditioned credit (`tr-vc-01`)

**Weakness.** A fixed price change read as variable consideration.

**What changes.** The same 10% credit. Only whether it depends on future usage decides.

**Deal context, both records.** Customer: enterprise software company on a three-year subscription at $960,000 per year, billed annually in advance. The vendor's standard order form has no credits.

**Questions, both records.**

- By name (`variable_consideration_concept`): Read with the deal information: under ASC 606, does `clause` involve variable consideration?
- Rule written out (`variable_consideration`): Can the amount the customer pays under `clause` change depending on a future event, such as usage, performance, or service levels? A fixed amount, or a fixed unit price on purchases the customer chooses to make, is not variable.

| | `tr-vc-01-a` | `tr-vc-01-b` |
|---|---|---|
| Clause | Annual Credit. For each of the first two contract years, Vendor will credit 10% of that year's fee ($96,000) against the next year's invoice. | Annual Credit. For each of the first two contract years, **if Customer's token usage in that year exceeds 600B tokens,** Vendor will credit 10% of that year's fee ($96,000) against the next year's invoice. |
| Facts that differ | `conditional_on_usage: false`; `usage_threshold_b: null` | `conditional_on_usage: true`; `usage_threshold_b: 600` |
| Correct answer | **No** to both questions | **Yes** to both questions |
| Rationale | Both credits are certain and fixed at signing, and each lands on an invoice inside the term. They lower the transaction price by $192,000, but nothing about them depends on a future event, so they are not variable consideration. | The credit depends on future usage, so the amount the customer pays is uncertain at signing: variable consideration to estimate and possibly constrain (606-10-32-5 to 32-9). |

**Label rule** (`scripts/build-training.ts`): yes when `conditional_on_usage`.

**Held-out evidence.**

- `rr-f4-h04` (Amendment): rule 0.59–0.64, gold no
- `rr-f1-h05` (Volume Add-On): rule 0.62–0.68, gold no
